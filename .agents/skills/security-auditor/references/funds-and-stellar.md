# Funds and Stellar

Start at `apps/backend/src/modules/{wallet,anchor,settlements,engine,sms,accounts}`, `apps/backend/src/infra/stellar`, `apps/backend/prisma`, `apps/mobile/src/services/{wallet,offline,sync,stellar}`, `contracts/src/lib.rs`, and `packages/pijin_core`. Follow active callers instead of relying on migration plans. Record each flow's initiator, signer, asset and network, balance of record, intermediate states, finality criterion, and recovery owner.

## Custody and transaction intent

- Inventory user, offline-device, gateway, relayer, issuer, treasury, registrar, contract upgrade, and deployment keys. Verify who generates, stores, uses, recovers, revokes, or rotates them. Check role separation, signing scope, thresholds, hot-wallet exposure, loss of a device or signer, and handover. Derive the custody model from actual signer authority and recovery behavior.
- Follow cash-in, cash-out, online transfers, vault deposits and withdrawals, offline spend, fees, funding and sponsorship, and any refund or manual adjustment. Compare the intent shown to the user with the artifact signed and the effects accepted by the backend and chain. Check amount, asset issuer or token address, source, destination and memo where relevant, network, contract, operation list, signer, sequence, expiry, and fee limits. Check external and direct Stellar submissions against the same asset and account policy.
- Check allowlists and environment separation, classic asset trustlines and relevant issuer authorization or clawback authority, transaction size and fee bounds, amount precision and rounding across TypeScript, database, XDR, and Rust, and authorization on token and contract calls.

## Conservation and settlement

- Reconcile actual custody balances and treasury movements against per-user claims by asset. Use the correct source of truth at each step. Check that debit, credit, fee, and refund effects are unique and conserved, balances cannot go below their allowed floor, and pending funds are excluded from spendable balances when required.
- Trace callback, queue, retry, direct submission, and scheduler paths to economically exactly-once effects. Check uniqueness scope and binding of idempotency keys to intent, database constraints and atomic transitions, lock scope and expiry, sequence allocation, worker concurrency, timeout after submission, crashes between durable writes and chain operations, and out-of-order or duplicate events.
- Verify that success requires matching authoritative chain or provider evidence for the correct intent. Check terminal status transitions, pending and unknown outcomes, retries versus permanent failures, stale records, missing events, reconciliation cadence and exception ownership. Confirm users and operators can distinguish pending, confirmed, failed, and reversed or refunded states. Assess exposure under a reconciliation backlog or provider outage.

## Offline vouchers and Soroban

- Trace the voucher bytes, signature validation, recipient resolution, and submission from mobile through SMS or sync, backend processing, and the contract. Compare canonical encoding and field binding across implementations. Check authorization, nonce scope, expiry and time source, replay across channels, devices, networks and contracts, offline-key registration and replacement, recipient ID mapping changes, local spending limits, and conflict handling when offline state becomes stale. Distinguish a locally issued voucher from confirmed settlement.
- Review every public contract entry point, including initialization, upgrade, recipient and gateway administration, offline-key registration, deposit, offline spend, withdrawal and queries. Verify effective Soroban authorization, signer and token boundaries, state-write ordering and rollback, replay state lifetime, storage TTL and restoration, arithmetic bounds, feature and privilege changes across upgrades, and resource exhaustion that could strand funds or block withdrawals.
- Verify release alignment where evidence exists. Compare contract ID, network, deployed WASM identity, privileged accounts, generated client and serialization, and mobile/backend release configuration. Mark source-only evidence as such. Review existing invariant and regression tests for conservation, unique effects across workers, grant and revocation behavior, expiry, and failed or pending settlement recovery.
